UK Retail Analytics Portfolio

↓

Business problem

No consolidated view of customer value, product margin, or regional performance across a 60,000-transaction retail dataset. Retention risks and loyalty tier mismatches were invisible to the business.

Data

60,000 transactions, 8,000 customers, 76 products, 20 stores, 11 UK regions spanning 2022–2024. Structured relational data across sales, product, and customer tables.

Work completed

12 SQL queries for extraction and segmentation. Python notebooks for RFM scoring, cohort retention analysis, and margin breakdown. Four-page Power BI dashboard covering trading performance, customer loyalty, regional breakdown, and channel analysis including DAX calculated measures.

Electronics delivered 54% of revenue at only 27.7% margin — significantly below the 51.4% margin achieved by Beauty & Health. Mobile App carried the highest AOV at £112, suggesting channel-specific promotional investment.

Predictive Delivery Time Analysis

PythonAdaBoostscikit-learn
↓

Business problem

Logistics operations lacked a data-driven model for predicting delivery times. Manual estimates failed to account for traffic density, weather, or distance interactions, leading to scheduling inefficiencies and customer satisfaction issues. A delay of even 5 minutes can drop customer satisfaction ratings by 30%.

Data

45,584 delivery records from Zomato with operational, environmental, and demographic features: distance, traffic density, weather conditions, time of day, vehicle condition, festival status, and courier ratings. Preprocessing included outlier removal, one-hot encoding, feature engineering (distance groupings, temporal attributes), and dataset splitting.

Work completed

Evaluated seven regression models: Linear Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest, AdaBoost, Gradient Boosting, SVR, and KNN. Applied GridSearchCV for hyperparameter tuning on AdaBoost and Gradient Boosting. Built and compared models across MSE, R², and accuracy metrics. Feature engineering included temporal attributes, distance groupings, and operational metrics. Manvendra’s individual contribution: AdaBoost Regressor and KNN (19% of overall project contribution).

Key findings & recommendations

AdaBoost Regressor achieved 83.59% accuracy (R² 0.83, MSE 14.94) — the strongest individual model in the group. KNN baseline: R² 0.65, MSE 31.60, demonstrating AdaBoost’s significant uplift. Traffic density, day of week, and festival status were the strongest predictors. Recommendation: integrate real-time traffic feeds into the prediction pipeline and implement dynamic delivery scheduling. Expanding data to include road closures and weather APIs would further improve accuracy. Individual models achieved 80–84% accuracy; shared KNN limited to 65–71%.

Q-Learning Agent — Taxi-v3 Reinforcement Learning

PythonQ-LearningOpenAI Gym
↓

Problem

Train a reinforcement learning agent to efficiently navigate the Taxi-v3 discrete environment (OpenAI Gym): picking up passengers from one of four locations and dropping them off at the correct destination, minimising steps and illegal moves.

Environment & setup

State space: 500 discrete states. Action space: 6 actions (N, S, E, W, pickup, dropoff). Q-Table shape (500, 6). Learning rate α=0.1, discount factor γ=0.99, ε-greedy exploration decaying from 1.0 to 0.05. 20,000 training episodes with a max of 200 steps each.

Work completed

Implemented tabular Q-learning algorithm with custom reward shaping: +5 for reaching pickup location, +5 for successful pickup, +1 for reducing Manhattan distance to goal (on top of base rewards). Added early stopping at 90% success rate over 100 episodes. Wrote evaluation script (eval.py) for post-training performance measurement.

Results & conclusions

Average reward (last 100 episodes): ~7.8, confirming strongly positive net performance above baseline. Success rate: ~93% — the agent consistently completed pickup and dropoff tasks in efficient step counts. Reward shaping significantly accelerated convergence and reduced inefficient exploration. Limitation: tabular Q-learning does not generalise to unseen states or continuous environments. Future work: Deep Q-Networks (DQN) for scalability to higher-dimensional environments.

Pruning for Efficiency in Biomedical Vision-Language Models

PyTorchLLM-PrunerLLaVA-Med
↓

Research problem

Biomedical vision-language models like LLaVA-Med v1.5 require substantial VRAM (15+ GB) and billions of parameters, making them inaccessible to resource-constrained hospitals and research institutions. The tension between accuracy and computational efficiency in biomedical AI remained underexplored. This dissertation investigated whether pruning strategies could reduce model size without undermining diagnostic reliability.

Data & benchmarks

Three publicly available biomedical VQA benchmarks used for calibration and evaluation (no patient-level data): PathVQA (large-scale pathology VQA), SLAKE (semantically enhanced biomedical QA), and PMC-VQA (figure-caption pairs from biomedical literature). Predefined train/validation/test splits retained; only test splits used for final evaluation.

Methodology

Applied structured (blockwise) pruning and attention-head pruning to LLaVA-Med v1.5 via the LLM-Pruner framework — selected for its dependency-aware algorithm that preserves inter-layer relationships and grouped-query attention constraints. Gradient-based sensitivity scores guided which blocks and heads to remove. No fine-tuning applied post-pruning, isolating the direct effect of compression. Evaluated using a dual framework: computational efficiency (VRAM, FLOPs, throughput, model size) and task performance (pred-relative score across benchmarks).

Results & implications

Parameters reduced from 7.57B to 6.46B (−14.63%). Model size reduced from 28,864 MB to 12,322 MB (−57.3%). VRAM at inference reduced from 15,211 MB to 13,099 MB (−13.88%). FLOPs reduced from 336.44B to 287.20B. Throughput improved from 10.55 to 11.98 tokens/second (+13.6%). Diagnostic accuracy (pred-relative score) declined only from 56.5% to 53.0% (−6.2%) — within acceptable bounds for research-stage biomedical AI. Implication: pruned model deployable on mid-tier GPUs (e.g. NVIDIA L4 24 GB) rather than requiring A100/H100, broadening clinical accessibility significantly.

Problem

Biomedical literature contains dense clinical terminology — disease names, drug entities, anatomical references — that is difficult to extract and classify automatically at scale. Data & tools

Standard biomedical NER benchmark datasets for entity recognition across clinical text. Fine-tuned BioBERT (pre-trained on PubMed and PMC corpora) using Hugging Face Transformers and PyTorch. Deployed on Hugging Face Spaces for public access.

Work completed

Results

F1 score of 0.8457 on biomedical NER benchmark — demonstrating strong entity detection and classification accuracy across clinical text. Model available on Hugging Face Spaces. This project directly informed the dissertation’s focus on biomedical language model efficiency.
